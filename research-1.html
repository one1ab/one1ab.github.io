Next Generation Semiconductors

Breaking through silicon scaling limits with atomically thin 2D semiconductor channels.

Why It Matters

Modern silicon transistors have shrunk to just a few nanometers, where unwanted leakage currents and heat make further scaling extraordinarily difficult. Atomically thin two-dimensional (2D) semiconductors—such as transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs: MoS₂, WS₂, WSe₂)—are less than one nanometer thick and have naturally dangling-bond-free surfaces, enabling ultra-sharp switching even at sub-5 nm channel lengths.

ONE 1ab Research Focus

  • High-mobility n-type and p-type 2D TMD transistors with low subthreshold swing
  • Low-resistance van der Waals contacts and interface trap passivation
  • Bias-stress reliability, variability reduction, and low-power CMOS compatibility

How It Works

  1. Atomically Thin Channel: A monolayer crystal (~0.7 nm) allows the gate electric field to control every charge carrier uniformly, suppressing short-channel leakage.
  2. Clean Interfaces: Because 2D layers bond via weak van der Waals forces, they stack cleanly with insulators and metals without surface roughness scattering.
  3. Contact & Dielectric Engineering: Tailoring metal–2D contacts and ultra-thin high-k gate dielectrics minimizes resistance and operating voltage.

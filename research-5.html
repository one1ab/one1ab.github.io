Emerging Nanoelectronics

Building atomic-scale Lego heterostructures to unlock new quantum and device physics.

Why It Matters

Traditional semiconductors can only be stacked if their crystal lattices match almost perfectly. 2D materials break this rule: any conductor, insulator, or semiconductor layer can be stacked like atomic-scale Lego bricks without lattice-matching constraints, opening entirely new device principles beyond conventional transistors.

ONE 1ab Research Focus

  • Vertical van der Waals heterostructures and tunneling nanoelectronic devices
  • Steep-slope, multi-valued logic (MVL), and electrostatically reconfigurable transistors
  • Fundamental charge transport and quantum interface physics at the nanoscale

How It Works

  1. Van der Waals Stacking: Combining graphene (metal), h-BN (insulator), and TMDs (semiconductors) creates atomically sharp vertical junctions.
  2. Quantum Tunneling & Steep Switching: Engineering barrier thickness at the single-atom level enables tunnel FETs, negative differential resistance (NDR), and multi-valued logic.
  3. Reconfigurable Functionality: Electrostatic gating can dynamically switch a single nano-device between n-type, p-type, diode, or memory modes.

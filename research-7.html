Flexible & Wearable Devices

Ultra-thin, bendable electronics that conform seamlessly to human skin and curved surfaces.

Why It Matters

Rigid silicon wafers crack as soon as they bend. By contrast, atomically thin 2D semiconductors can withstand remarkable mechanical strain while maintaining high electronic performance—making them the ultimate building blocks for skin-like patches, rollable displays, and next-generation wearable healthcare.

ONE 1ab Research Focus

  • Mechanically durable flexible 2D transistors, memories, and transparent circuits
  • Skin-conformal wearable patches for continuous physiological monitoring
  • Bending/fatigue reliability analysis under repeated mechanical deformation

How It Works

  1. Intrinsic Mechanical Flexibility: Because bending strain scales with thickness, sub-nanometer 2D sheets flex effortlessly without fracturing.
  2. Strain-Relieving Layouts: Serpentine interconnects and neutral-plane polymer encapsulation protect circuits across thousands of bending and stretching cycles.
  3. Low-Temperature Processing: Transfer and low-thermal-budget fabrication enable high-performance electronics directly on polyimide, PET, or biocompatible elastomers.

Real-World Applications

High-speed, ultra-thin transparent display backplanes
